We agree that our earlier reconstruction and exhaustive-search arguments did not establish lower bounds on the best attack against QEF. We have removed the claimed fixed attack exponents and the associated "passed" classifications.

The revised analysis instead uses the structure of QEF's pixel-controlled Euler rotations. We derive their first and conjugation moments under explicitly stated finite-angle and continuous reference laws. These moments give a bound on the average ciphertext's distance and relative entropy to the maximally mixed state. Holevo's theorem and binary Fano's inequality then yield a lower bound on the number of independently encrypted preparations required to distinguish two specified images at a prescribed success probability, allowing an arbitrary joint measurement.

The revised table is explicitly a conditional analytical benchmark. It is not an elementary-gate attack cost, a measured attack result, or a certification of the original generators. The derivation also includes a source-imperfection parameter and a contrasting angle-range example demonstrating why the generator law cannot be omitted.

We have clarified that keys are refreshed independently for each preparation and delivered securely to the receiver. Pairs from different operations therefore are not assumed to sample one fixed encryption unitary. Neither full-key recovery nor arbitrary-unitary reconstruction is treated as a necessary step in a plaintext attack. The abstract, discussion, and conclusion now reflect these limits. Certifying the required joint angle law for the original nD-QHCS/HG-Phi sources remains an explicit outstanding requirement.
